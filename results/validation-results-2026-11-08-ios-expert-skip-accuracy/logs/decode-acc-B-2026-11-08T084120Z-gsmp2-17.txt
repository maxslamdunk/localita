- Speed relationship: Sidewalk speed ($v_s$) = 3 $\times$ Beach speed ($v_b$)

**2. Calculate the current speed on the beach:**
The time spent on the beach is given as 56 minutes.
The distance on the beach is 14 km.
Speed = Distance / Time.
However, since we want the final answer in minutes, it is easier to work with "kilometers per minute" or simply use the relationship between speed and time.
$v_b = \frac{14 \text{ km}}{56 \text{ min}} = 2.5 \text{ km/min}$ (Wait, this is a very high speed for walking. Let's re-evaluate the units. If $v_b$ is speed, then $t_b = d_b / v_b$. $56 = 14 / v_b \implies v_b = 14/56 = 0.25$ km/min).

**3. Determine the speed on the sidewalk:**
The problem states that the sidewalk speed is three times the beach speed.
$v_s = 3 \times v_b$
$v_s = 3 \times 0.25 \text{ km/min} = 0.75 \text{ km/min}$.

**4. Calculate the time spent on the sidewalk:**
The distance on the sidewalk is 18 km.
$t_s = \frac{d_s}{v_s}$
$t_s = \frac{18 \text{ km}}{0.75 \text{ km/min}}$
$t_s = \frac{18}{3/4} = 18 \times \frac{4}{3} = 6 \times 4 = 24$ minutes.

**5. Calculate the total time:**
Total time = Time on beach + Time on sidewalk
Total time = $t_b + t_s$
Total time = $56 \text{ minutes} + 24 \text{ minutes} = 80 \text{ minutes}$.
