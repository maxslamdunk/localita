The city is 72 miles across. We need to find how long it takes to cover a quarter of the city.
Target distance = $\frac{1}{4} \times 72 \text{ miles} = 18 \text{ miles}$.

**Step 3: Calculate the time taken to reach the target distance.**
We need to check if the wind speed change (which happens in one hour) affects the calculation for the first 18 miles.
In one hour (60 minutes), at the initial speed:
Distance covered in 60 minutes = $60 \text{ minutes} \times get 0.1 \text{ miles/minute} = 6 \text{ miles}$.

Since the target distance is 18 miles, the wind speed change will occur after 6 miles have been covered.

**Step 4: Calculate the time for the remaining distance after the speed change.**
After 60 minutes, the wind speed is halved.
New speed ($v_2$) = $\frac{0.1 \text{ miles/minute}}{2} = 0.05 \text{ miles/minute}$.

The remaining distance to reach the target is:
Remaining distance = $18 \text{ miles} - 6 \text{ miles} = 12 \text{ miles}$.

Time to cover the remaining distance at the new speed:
Time = $\frac{\text{Distance}}{\text{Speed}} = \frac{12 \text{ miles}}{0.05 \text{ miles/minute}} = 240 \text{ minutes}$.

**Step 5: Calculate the total time.**
Total time = Initial time + Time for remaining distance
Total time = $60 \text{ minutes} + 240 \text{ minutes} = 300 \text{ minutes}$.
