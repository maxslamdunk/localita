To solve this problem, we need to determine the total time Emma takes to complete her walk based on the given distances, speeds, and the time spent on the beach.

**1. Identify the given information:**
- Total distance: 32 km
- Beach distance ($d_b$): 14 km
- Sidewalk distance ($d_s$): 18 km
- Speed relationship: Sidewalk speed ($v_s$) = 3 $\times$ Beach speed ($v_b$)
- Injury effect: Speeds are halved (this affects both, so the ratio remains the same).
- Time spent on beach ($t_b$): 56 minutes.

**2. Calculate the current speed on the beach:**
The time spent on the beach is given as 56 minutes for 14 km.
$v_b = \frac{d_b}{t_b} = \frac{14 \text{ km}}{56 \text{ min}} = 0.25 \text{ km/min}$.

**3. Determine the speed on the sidewalk:**
The problem states that the sidewalk speed is three times the beach speed.
$v_s = _3 \times v_b = 3 \times 0.25 \text{ km/min} = 0.75 \text{ km/min}$.

*Note: The problem mentions that her speed decreased by half due to an injury. Since the 56 minutes is the time spent on the beach **after** the injury (as it is part of her current walk), the 0.25 km/min is her current speed. The ratio of 3:1 remains constant regardless of the injury.*

**4. Calculate the time spent on the sidewalk:**
The distance on the sidewalk is 18 km.
$t_s = \frac{d_s}{v_s} = \frac{18 \text{ km}}{0.75 \text{ km/min}}$.
$t_s = \frac{18}{3/4} = 18 \times \frac{4}{3} = 6 \times 4 = 24 \text{ minutes}$.

**5. Calculate the total time for the walk:**
Total time ($T$) = Time on beach ($t_b$) + Time on sidewalk ($t_s$).
$T = 56 \text{ minutes} + 24 \text{ minutes} = 80 \text{ minutes}$.

Final answer: 80
